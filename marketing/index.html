The scanner

Text recognition, a pixel check, and the number printed on the card.

Point the camera and the card resolves itself. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is queued for a server, and it all still works in aeroplane mode.

On device

OCR that never phones home

The text engine is self-hosted and runs in the browser. It reads the card name and the collector line, and a pixel check spots foil sheen.

Any language

The collector line rescues the read

When a name can't be read, the printed numbers can: Magic by set and number, Yu-Gi-Oh! by its 8-digit passcode, Pokémon by a multi-language sweep.

Slabs

Graded holders read as themselves

PSA, BGS, SGC and CGC labels read as company, grade, qualifier and cert. A PSA 10 is tracked as its own copy and never merges with your raw one. On PSA holders the cert can resolve to the exact card, quota permitting.

Whole pages

Scan a binder page at a time

Up to twelve card regions are found in one photo, cropped and identified one by one, then reviewed before anything is added. Uploading a photo beats scanning a page live, and the app tells you so.

Sports cards

No catalog exists, so the card is built from what's printed on it.

No free API publishes the set of printed sports cards. The scanner reads the year, brand, set, card number, player, parallel and serial numbering, and constructs the card from that — on device, like everything else.

Honest by design

Your figure, not ours

Sports cards carry no prices. Value is the number you set, next to a one-tap eBay sold-comps link built from every attribute the scanner read.

Guarded

It would rather not guess

A misread here would invent a card that doesn't exist, so a confidence floor and closed vocabularies keep it from doing that.

Collection & decks

A portfolio that happens to be made of cardboard.

Value

Cost basis, P&L, movers

Portfolio value with 30-day insights, priced per finish and condition. CSV import and export, JSON backups.

Decks

Built by hand, or by model

Mana curve, colour and type stats, and owned-versus-missing costing that won’t let two decks both claim the same copy. The AI builder — which researches the current meta and designs around seed cards you pick — is part of the subscription.

Binders

Binders that exist on a shelf

Custom binders with their own audience, page numbers stamped by a page scan, and a printed QR label that opens the right binder from any phone camera.

Friends & trades

Sharing works with nobody signed in.

A share link is the data — your binder compressed into the URL — so nothing is published anywhere unless you send it to someone.

Mark what's for trade

Set how many copies of a card you're willing to move. It never exceeds what you own.

Send a link or a file

Your binder, or your whole collection, travels as a link. Wants ride along, so both sides see matches highlighted.

Propose card by card

Both sides priced. The reply travels back the same way, and booking the trade updates both inventories.

Or claim a handle

Optional. Friends add you by @handle, offers arrive in the app, and binders refresh themselves.

Built to be checked

A scanner is only as good as the battery you point at it.

Card identification is graded against committed real imagery, not vibes. These are counts from the repository, not estimates.

Fixtures

289 real photographs

44 MB of hand-curated card photos, binder pages and handheld video frames, committed to the repo — because a photograph can’t be regenerated.

Degradations

18 seeded camera faults

Soft focus, rotation, perspective, glare, low light and foil sheen, applied from a fixed seed so any two runs are comparable cell by cell.

Unit tests

395 cases, 1,001 assertions

Including the QR encoder, decoded by a third-party decoder at every version the app emits — the label ends up glued to a binder.

Harnesses

19 test entry points

Camera release, the built bundle booting, and live database checks proving a stranger can’t read a friends-only binder or a private conversation.

Sources

6 upstream catalogs

Scryfall, pokemontcg.io, TCGdex, YGOPRODeck, Lorcast and the TCGplayer catalog — across nine games, plus sealed product for every one of them.

Shipping

~200 KB gzipped

Three caches: a build-keyed shell, card art capped at 480 images, and the text engine. Prices are never cached — a stale price is worse than no price.

Identification rates are measured per battery and published in the repo’s docs. We don’t quote a single headline number, because a battery of stills can’t bound how a live scanner behaves.

What we can and can't see

Everything lives on your device until you decide otherwise.

  • Your collection is stored in your browser. Scanning, collection, decks and link-based sharing need no account and no server, and work offline.
  • Camera frames are processed on your device. One opt-in extra — cloud rescue, off unless you switch it on — sends a single frame when a scan is stuck, and that is the only path by which a picture of a card leaves the device.
  • Sign in and your collection is backed up automatically. It is encrypted at rest with a key our server mints, which makes it protected storage and not end-to-end encryption — we could read it, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise.
  • Google Drive backup is separate and opt-in, into a private folder in your Drive that nothing else can enumerate. Your browser talks to Google directly.
  • A published binder is plaintext to us, because a friend’s app has to read it. A “public” binder means any signed-in collector — never the open web.
  • Diagnostics are content-free — no card names, no queries, no keys, never tied to your account — and are on by default outside the EU, EEA and UK, where you’re asked first.
