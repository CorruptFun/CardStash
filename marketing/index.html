Scanning

Point, price, collect.

Hover the camera over a card and its price pops up live. Tap once and it’s yours — the right printing, the right finish, the right condition.

Camera to collection

No typing, no set codes to look up. Scan a stack one after another and file the whole lot in a single pass when you’re done.

Photograph a whole binder page

Up to twelve cards from one photo. You review the list before anything is added, and the app remembers which page each card came from.

Slabs and sealed product too

Scan a PSA, BGS, SGC or CGC holder and the grade is read off the label. A PSA 10 is tracked as its own copy, never merged with your raw one. Sealed boxes and packs count as themselves until you open them.

Cards no catalog has

Regional promos, prereleases, error prints. Photograph it, type what it is, and it lives in your collection like anything else.

Your portfolio

What it’s worth, what it cost, what moved.

A collection is an asset whether or not you treat it like one. Cardstock treats it like one.

Priced the way you own it

Foil and non-foil priced separately, then adjusted for condition — a played copy isn’t counted at near-mint money.

Cost basis and profit & loss

Enter what you paid and see what it’s done since, per card and across everything.

A 30-day line and your movers

Which cards gained, which slipped, and by how much in real money — not percentages of a card worth forty cents.

Bring the collection you already have

Import a CSV from your old tracker — only a name column is required — and export everything back out whenever you like. It’s your list.

Sharing & trading

Cards are for trading. So share them.

Put up a binder of what you’ll part with, heart the cards you’re hunting, and let the matching do the introductions.

Mark what’s for trade

Say how many copies you’ll move. Keep the rest of your collection to yourself.

Share your binder

Send it as a link to anyone, or print a QR label and stick it on the real binder’s spine so the right list opens from a phone camera.

See both sides of the match

Your want list travels with your binder, so you both see it at once: their cards you’re after, your cards they’re after.

Book it and move on

Trade card by card with both sides priced. When it’s done, both collections update themselves.

Make an account

One minute, and your collection stops living on a single phone.

There is no sign-up form. Your email address is the account — we send a six-digit code, you type it in, and you’re there. No password to invent, nothing to confirm later.

It backs itself up as you go

No switch to find, no passphrase to remember. Sign in on a second device and your cards come down to it by themselves.

A name people can trade with

Claim an @handle and friends add you by typing it. Their binders then refresh themselves, and offers land in the app instead of in a file somebody has to remember to send.

Find the card you’re hunting

Your want list gets matched against every collector offering that card, in any printing. That is the one thing a shared link can never do for you.

Talk to the collector directly

Message someone about a specific card and settle the whole trade between yourselves. That costs nothing, and it always will.

Straight answers

Do I have to sign in?
No. Scanning, your collection, decks, binders and link sharing all work with nothing signed in, and they work offline.
What does signing in change?
Your collection is copied to our server, so a lost phone isn’t a lost collection. It’s encrypted at rest with a key we hold — that makes it protected storage, not end-to-end encryption, and we’d rather say so than imply otherwise.
Does an account publish my cards?
No. Claiming a handle publishes nothing. Putting a binder up is a separate switch, and you choose who can see it.
Can I get my collection out again?
Always. CSV and JSON export, whenever you like, account or not.
